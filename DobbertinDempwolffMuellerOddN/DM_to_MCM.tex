\section{The MCM Polynomial}

Let $F=\F_{2^n}$ and define the truncated trace
\[
  \Trunc_m(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}x^{2^i}.
\]
The M\"uller--Cohen--Matthews polynomial formalized in the project is
\[
  \MCM_{n,m}(x)=\Trunc_m(x)^{2^m+1}x^{(2^n-1)-2^m}.
\]
For $x\ne0$, this is the quotient
\[
  \MCM_{n,m}(x)=\frac{\Trunc_m(x)^{2^m+1}}{x^{2^m}},
\]
because $x^{2^n-1}=1$.  The polynomial form gives the intended value at zero.

\begin{theorem}[MCM permutation theorem]
Assume $0<m<n$, $m$ and $n$ are odd, and $\gcd(m,n)=1$.  Then
$\MCM_{n,m}$ is a permutation of $F$.
\end{theorem}

This is the Lean theorem \texttt{DempwolffMueller.MCM.mcm\_permutation}, in
\texttt{DobbDempMue/DempwolffMueller2013/MCM.lean}.

\section{Two Permutation Inputs}

The proof combines a Dempwolff--M\"uller permutation with a Gold permutation.

\subsection{Dempwolff--M\"uller transfer}

For $1<m<n$, $m$ odd, and $\gcd(m,n)=1$, let $k'$ satisfy
\[
  (2^{n-1}-2^{m-1}-1)k'\equiv2^{m-1}\pmod{2^n-1}.
  \tag{DM}
\]
Then
\[
  D_{k'}(x)=\Trunc_m(x)x^{k'}
\]
is a permutation of $F$.  In Lean this is
\texttt{dempwolff\_mueller\_permutation\_ktransfer}, which reuses
\texttt{DempwolffMueller.LxXk'\_bijective}.

\subsection{Gold}

Under the oddness and coprimality hypotheses, the Gold map
\[
  G(y)=y^{2^m+1}
\]
is a permutation of $F$.  This is \texttt{gold\_permutation}, reusing
\texttt{KasamiAPN.gold\_pow\_bijective}.

\section{The Linking Exponent}

The key arithmetic lemma \texttt{KasamiAPN.exists\_linking\_exp} supplies an
exponent $k'$ satisfying both \textup{(DM)} and
\[
  k'(2^m+1)\equiv(2^n-1)-2^m\pmod{2^n-1}.
  \tag{MCM}
\]
The second congruence gives, for every $x\in F$,
\[
  x^{(2^n-1)-2^m}=x^{k'(2^m+1)}.
\]
It follows that
\begin{align*}
  \MCM_{n,m}(x)
    &=\Trunc_m(x)^{2^m+1}x^{k'(2^m+1)}\\
    &=\bigl(\Trunc_m(x)x^{k'}\bigr)^{2^m+1}\\
    &=G(D_{k'}(x)).
\end{align*}
The equality, including the zero case, is formalized as
\texttt{MCM\_eq\_dempwolff\_mueller\_pow}.

\section{Proof When $m>1$}

Choose $k'$ from the linking-exponent lemma.  By \textup{(DM)}, $D_{k'}$ is
bijective.  The Gold theorem makes $G$ bijective.  Since
\[
  \MCM_{n,m}=G\circ D_{k'},
\]
the MCM polynomial is bijective as a composition of bijections.  The final Lean
step uses \texttt{Function.Bijective.comp} after rewriting the function by the
factorization theorem.

\section{The Case $m=1$}

The transfer theorem requires $1<m$, but the MCM polynomial is already the
identity for $m=1$.  Indeed, $\Trunc_1(x)=x$, and
\begin{align*}
  \MCM_{n,1}(x)
    &=x^{2^1+1}x^{(2^n-1)-2}\\
    &=x^{2^n}\\
    &=x.
\end{align*}
The last equality is the finite-field Frobenius identity.  The Lean proof uses
\texttt{FiniteField.pow\_card} and concludes with
\texttt{Function.bijective\_id}.
